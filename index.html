01About01 / 08

Research with a view beyond the obvious

Research with a view beyond the obvious. His work begins with a practical question: how do organisations decide what to make, move and measure, and what do those decisions cost over time?

Much of the answer has been sought in Indian settings. Pharmaceutical supply chains under pressure to become greener. Consumers weighing trust against convenience when they buy medicines online. Firms that ask for innovation and then quietly discourage it.

At Dayananda Sagar College of Engineering he brings the same habits to teaching and to the institute's innovation and entrepreneurship work: define the problem carefully, test it against evidence, and stay curious about what the evidence leaves unexplained.

02Research areas02 / 08

Six themes, one line of enquiry.

Areas of interest that recur across his teaching, publications and project work. Each asks how better evidence leads to better decisions.

01

  1. 01

    Operations & Analytics

    How processes, capacity and quality are planned and measured, and how data sharpens the decisions behind them.

  2. 02

    Sustainable Supply Chains

    Green practice across sourcing, production and distribution, and what drives or enables its adoption in an emerging economy.

  3. 03

    Circular Economy

    Keeping materials in use for longer: reverse flows, recovery, and designing waste out of supply networks.

  4. 04

    Organisational Innovation

    Why capable organisations still stall on new ideas, and the structures and habits that help or hinder them.

  5. 05

    Digital Transformation & AI

    How firms and consumers adopt new technology, and where AI tools can support sounder managerial judgement.

  6. 06

    Corporate Governance & Sustainability

    How oversight, accountability and disclosure shape responsible performance over the long term.

Faculty Coordinator — IEDC, DSCE

Research earns its keep when someone can act on it. At DSCE, that conviction takes shape through the Innovation & Entrepreneurship Development Center.

The IEDC and the DSCE Pre-Incubation Center give students a place to test an idea before it has to be a business: to frame a problem, build a first version, and hear honest feedback. His association with the centre connects that practice to the discipline of operations and analytics.
